ISO 3103

The content of this page has been slightly adapted from the ISO 3103 article on Wikipedia.

ISO 3103 is a standard published by the International Organization for Standardization describing a method for making tea, published in 1980 and amended in 1999.

The abstract states the following:

The method consists in extracting of soluble substances in dried tea leaves in a porcelain or glazed earthenware pot, pouring freshly boiling water onto the leaves, letting the brew steep for six minutes, and pouring the tea into a bowl (also white porcelain or glazed earthenware) before measuring its properties.

this standard is not meant to define the proper method for brewing tea, but rather a method for brewing a tea sample for sensory comparison, such as in a taste test.

The work was the winner of the parodic Ig Nobel Prize for Literature in 1999.

To maintain consistent results, the following are recommendations given by the standard:
  • The pot should be white porcelain or glazed earthenware and have a partly serrated edge. It should have a lid that fits loosely inside the pot.
  • The pot can be large or small, with a prescribed volume and weight.
    • Pot Volume Weight
    large 310 ml (±8 ml) 200 g (±10 g)
    small 150 ml (±4 ml) 118 g (±10 g)
  • 2 grams of tea (measured to ±2% accuracy) per 100 ml boiling water is placed into the pot.
  • Freshly boiling water is poured into the pot to within 4-6 mm of the brim. Allow 20 seconds for water to cool.
  • The water should be similar to the drinking water where the tea will be consumed.
  • Brewing time is six minutes.
  • The brewed tea is then poured into a white porcelain or glazed earthenware bowl.

The bowl can be large or small, with a prescribed capacity and weight.

Bowl Capacity Weight
large 380 ml 200 g (±20 g)
small 200 ml 105 g (±20 g)

If the test involves milk, then it is added to the bowl before pouring the infused tea into it, unless that is contrary to the organisation's normal practice.

If milk is added after the pouring of tea, the standard notes that best results are obtained when the liquid is between 65-80 °C.

5 ml of milk for the large bowl, or 2.5 ml for the small bowl, is used.

Pot and bowl

An annex of the standard describes two alternative pots (310 ml and 150 ml) and corresponding bowls (380 ml and 200 ml) "which are in widespread use" for tea tasting, including engineering drawings of their cross sections. The type of pot described is also known as a taster's mug.

Criticism

The protocol has been criticized for omitting any mention of prewarming the pot. Ireland was the only country to object, and objected on technical grounds.

Competing standards

In 2003, the Royal Society of Chemistry published a press release entitled "How to make a Perfect Cup of Tea".
